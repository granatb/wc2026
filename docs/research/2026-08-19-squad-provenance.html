1The pipeline, step by step

1

Match odds → expected goals MARKET

DraftKings 1X2 + over/under for each GW1 fixture (ESPN feed, core/fpl_odds.py). The bookmaker margin is removed (de-vig), then a Dixon-Coles solver finds the pair of team expected-goals (λ) that reproduces those market probabilities.

P(home), P(draw), P(away), P(over 2.5)  →  solve  →  (λ_home, λ_away, ρ)

GW1 result: ARS 2.56 v COV 0.56 · MCI 2.35 v BOU 1.12 · HUL 0.79 v MUN 2.18 … (10/10 fixtures priced).

2

Future gameweeks → calibrated FDR prior CALIBRATED

No book prices GW2+ yet (verified: ESPN carries zero GW2 prices and no EPL futures markets). Instead, FPL’s own fixture-difficulty ratings (all 380 fixtures, in the official API) are mapped to λ by fitting on the 10 GW1 fixtures where both FDR and market odds exist:

log(λ) = 1.056 − 0.242×FDR + 0.075×home   (mean abs error 22% on GW1)

These are priors, not prices (stored with source: fdr_prior…). Each week the newly priced GW replaces its prior, and solved team strengths will take over as real data accumulates.

3

Player priors FPL API PROXY

From the official FPL API: last season’s per-90 xG and xA (each player’s share of his club’s attack), penalty and set-piece duty, DefCon rates (backfilled per player, 400 calls), prices, ownership. Start probability is the weak link: it is a price-rank proxy within each club, not team news. 163 players with no PL history sit on price-based cold-start priors.

4

Research overlay NOTES

Markdown notes (round-scoped) override start probability and nudge λ per player, blended at weight 0.30. 12 GW1 notes now exist, derived from the expert corpus (6 asserted starters up, 6 World-Cup-hangover risks down), each carrying its sources and marked pending owner review. Press-conference notes on Thursday/Friday supersede them.

5

Monte-Carlo simulation

20–50k simulated gameweeks: goals arrive by team λ, distributed to players by their shares and sampled minutes; every FPL scoring rule applies per sim, including the BPS table ranked within each match (bonus is competitive — a strength no regression model has).

6

Selection

Players ranked by simulated expected points over the GW1–6 horizon (later weeks discounted 1.00→0.78 for prior uncertainty); squads assembled by a greedy formation-sweep knapsack under £100.0m / quotas / max 3 per club. Known gap: greedy, not an exact MILP solve.

7The shield call, in plain terms

Haaland is ~74% owned. When a player this popular hauls and you do not own him, roughly three quarters of the field gains on you at once; owning him neutralizes that swing (a shield). Pure EV says his £15.5 buys more points spread across three players — forcing him in costs about 1–2.5 expected points in GW1. So the choice is: pay ~2 points of expectation as insurance against a rank shock, or take the EV and accept the variance. It is a risk-preference decision, not a model output. (Same logic scaled by captaincy: his effective ownership with armbands exceeds 100%.)

Resolved 2026-08-19 (owner): the two squads split the question instead of compromising on it. The consensus squad owns Haaland (c) — following the crowd, shield included, is its entire strategy. The model squad holds the pure-EV line: he is not our guy at £15.5. Neither squad hedges its philosophy; the weekly scoreboard between them is the argument.

8FPL is not the World Cup game

The WC engine assumed weekly flexibility: full squad rebuilds between rounds, odds always available a few days ahead, six one-shot rounds. FPL inverts all three:

World Cup gamesFPL
Squad changesRebuild every round1 free transfer/week (bank up to 5, −4 per extra)
Decision horizonThis round onlyGW1–6+ — the initial 15 is a 6-week commitment
Odds coverageAlways priced pre-roundCurrent GW only; futures must be derived
One-week spikesGreat picksTraps (you cannot ditch them next week for free)
Season length8 rounds38 GWs: chips, price ladder, rank game theory

Architecture rule (owner, 2026-08-19): the core stays shared where it genuinely fits (sim engine, odds math, scoring assembly); anything FPL needs that the WC never did — team-strength persistence, a real minutes model, a multi-GW transfer optimizer — is built properly as an FPL module, never shimmed. Prediction quality outranks architectural reuse.
